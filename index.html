FOR A FORGING COMPANY

Where should we play?

Do not bet the plant on engine parts. Build a powertrain-neutral portfolio first, then earn entry into EV-specific precision forgings.

PRIORITY 01 · ENTER NOW

Chassis and wheel-end

Steering knuckles, control arms, wheel hubs, tie-rod ends, yokes, and axle components work across ICE, hybrid, and EV platforms.

  • Largest addressable vehicle base
  • Safety-critical value supports better margins
  • Aluminum forging can answer EV weight pressure
Strategic fit: 9 / 10
PRIORITY 02 · BUILD CAPABILITY

E-drive precision parts

Motor shafts, e-axle shafts, reduction gears, and differential components replace part of the traditional transmission value pool.

  • Fast-growing local EV customer base
  • Requires precision machining partnerships
  • Qualification barrier protects incumbents
Strategic fit: 8 / 10
PRIORITY 03 · DEFEND SELECTIVELY

ICE and hybrid cranktrain

Continue crankshafts, connecting rods, and transmission parts where tooling is paid down and programs include exports or hybrids.

  • Strong near-term cash generation
  • Hybrid extends useful program life
  • Long-term volume concentration risk
Strategic fit: 6 / 10
24-MONTH ENTRY PLAN

From forge shop to qualified automotive supplier

The commercial bottleneck is qualification, not only press capacity.

  1. 0–3 monthsChoose two beachhead parts

    Start with one powertrain-neutral steel part and one lightweight or EV-specific part. Map annual volume, incumbent import price, alloy, tolerance, and validation burden.

  2. 3–6 monthsClose the quality-system gap

    Build toward IATF 16949, APQP, PPAP, FMEA, MSA, SPC, traceability, and CQI-9 heat-treatment controls. Confirm ISO 14001 expectations with target OEMs.

  3. 4–9 monthsCreate a complete process cell

    Combine simulation, die design, controlled billet heating, forging, heat treatment, shot blast, machining, NDT, dimensional inspection, and fatigue validation.

  4. 6–15 monthsCo-develop with Tier 1 customers

    Approach suppliers around Hải Phòng, Hà Nội, Thái Nguyên, Quảng Nam, and HCMC clusters with costed localization proposals—not a generic capability deck.

  5. 12–24 monthsWin PPAP, then scale

    Run prototype and pilot lots, prove Cpk and fatigue life, secure nominated volume, and preserve dual-use export capacity to reduce dependence on one Vietnamese OEM.

AI + AUTOMATION BLUEPRINT

Build a forge that
learns every cycle.

Automation should stabilize the process. AI should explain variation, predict failure, and help people make faster decisions. Start with traceable data—not a large language model.

OPERATING PRINCIPLESense → Control → LearnKeep safety interlocks and final disposition under deterministic controls and accountable human authority.
01

Connect the process

Capture billet ID, alloy heat, furnace zone temperature, soak time, transfer time, press force curve, die ID, lubrication, trim parameters, heat-treatment recipe, inspection, and operator.

Foundation: historian + MES + part genealogy
02

Automate repeatability

Use robots for billet handling, transfer, lubrication, trimming, shot blast loading, machine tending, and palletizing. Use PLCs for interlocks and recipes.

Foundation: safe deterministic control
03

Add bounded AI

Train models on a specific part family and failure mode. Deploy advisory mode first, validate against holdout production, then permit controlled closed-loop adjustments.

Foundation: monitored, versioned models

REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE

Keep control local and learning connected

Do not connect an AI model directly to press safety logic. Separate operational technology from enterprise systems and expose only governed data pathways.

Level 0–1Sensors · robots · PLC · safety→Level 2SCADA · recipes · edge inference→Level 3MES · QMS · genealogy · historian→Level 4ERP · BI · model training
RECOMMENDED FIRST PILOT

One press, one part, one defect family

  1. Baseline: eight weeks of scrap, downtime, inspection, energy, and cycle-time data.
  2. Instrument: synchronized press force, billet temperature, die cycles, and controlled vision.
  3. Label: quality engineers define a defect taxonomy and verify training labels.
  4. Shadow: run predictions without changing production; measure false accepts and false rejects.
  5. Gate: deploy only if capability and business thresholds are met; preserve manual fallback.

Targets are management gates, not guaranteed outcomes. Establish the baseline before fixing exact commitments.

People and ownership

Create a small manufacturing intelligence team: process engineer as product owner, controls engineer, quality/metrology lead, maintenance lead, data engineer, and trained line champions. AI does not own product release.

Model controls

Version data, labels, code, model, threshold, and deployment. Record every prediction and override. Monitor drift by part, die, material heat, shift, and camera condition.

Cyber and safety

Segment OT networks, use least privilege, signed updates, backups, and a tested manual fallback. PLC and safety systems remain authoritative for machine protection.

Buy vs. build

Buy cameras, condition monitoring, MES, and connectivity where mature. Build only the process-specific models and decision logic that encode your forging knowledge.
